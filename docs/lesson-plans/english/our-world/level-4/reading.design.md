## Objectives (LP verbatim)

> Students will
> - **understand the effects of force on roller coasters and bicycles.**
> - **describe a roller-coaster ride.**

- **Reading Strategy:** Understand Cause and Effect
- **Academic Language:** describe
- **Content Vocabulary:** safety bar, steep, centripetal force, inertia, experience
- **Graphic Organizer:** two-column chart (Cause | Effect)

## Hard scope line

Cause and effect only — *because*, *why*, *as a result*. The LP's Be The Expert box
names exactly those three signal words. **No** *so that*, **no** conditionals beyond the
passage's own "If it stops quickly…", **no** passive voice drills. Sequence signal
words belong to Unit 8 Reading and must not reappear as the strategy here.

## The spine

**Build the Force Map of the ride.** A five-slot roller-coaster track sits on screen
from slide 3 and fills one slot per paragraph: **safety bar → steep hill → loop the
loop → brakes → every day**. Each slot only unlocks when Leo names the force at
work *and* its effect, so the cause-and-effect strategy is the only tool that moves
the car along the track.

- **Load-bearing** — a slot cannot open without a correct cause→effect pair.
- **Accumulates** — the track is visible throughout and never resets; by the end it
  is a complete annotated ride.
- **Earned payoff** — the final slide runs the whole ride back with all four forces
  captioned, which is exactly the LP's formative task ("use gravity, centripetal
  force, inertia, and friction to describe a roller-coaster ride").
- **Threads the LP verbatim** — the Warm Up force-mime, the ¶-pair comprehension
  checks, Activity 2's four questions, Activity 3's force chart, the Extend
  cause/effect chart, and Activity 4's dialogue are each a spine beat, not a
  decorative drill.

## The SB passage — verbatim (SB pp.156–157, TR 9.8)

> **Up, Down, and All Around!**
>
> ¶1 You are going on a roller-coaster ride. Sit in the car and pull down the safety bar. Are you ready? Let's go!
>
> ¶2 First you go up a steep hill. The roller coaster goes slow. Next gravity pulls you down the hill. The roller coaster moves fast. You feel very light!
>
> ¶3 A big circle, known as the "loop the loop," is many people's favorite. When you go fast up the circle, you feel heavy. Gravity is pulling you down. When you reach the top, you are high in the sky. And you're hanging upside down! So why don't you fall out of your seat? Your body wants to fly off, but the speed of the car and a force called centripetal force keep you moving in a circle, and keep you in your seat!
>
> ¶4 The roller coaster uses friction to stop. If it stops quickly, your body wants to continue moving. That is called inertia. But the safety bars keep you in place!
>
> ¶5 Not everyone loves roller coasters. They make some people feel dizzy or sick because the forces change. In fact, we experience forces like friction, centripetal force, and gravity every day. For example, you feel gravity when you jump with your bike and come down. You feel centripetal force when you turn, and you feel friction when you use the brakes to stop the bike.
>
> ¶6 How do you want to experience the forces? Do you want to ride your bicycle or a roller coaster?

**Weird but True (verbatim):** Russians invented the roller coaster. They made the "cars" from ice. And they put straw on the seats to keep the passengers warm.

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|

| centripetal force | you go in a circle |

### ¶-pair comprehension checks (LP p.306, verbatim)

- ¶1–2: What force pulls a roller coaster down a hill? → **gravity**
- ¶3–4: What force keeps people in their seats when the roller coaster is upside down? → **centripetal force**
- ¶5–6: What force do you feel when you use brakes to stop a bike? → **friction**

### Activity 4 — Wrap Up dialogue (SB p.157, verbatim)

- *"What happens first?"* → *"You go straight toward the sky, but then you turn!"*
- *"What does the roller coaster look like?"* → *"It looks like a spaceship!"*

## The WB passage — verbatim (WB p.112, TR 9.5)

> **The Science of Skateboarding**
>
> Skateboards are much more than just four wheels connected to a piece of wood. Let's look at the science of the fun! To understand more, we need to study three important parts of the skateboard: the deck, the truck, and the wheels.
>
> The **deck** is the board. It is usually made of maple wood, which is very strong but can bend, too. Modern skateboard decks have curves at the "nose" (the front) and the "tail" (the back). After you push and the wheels start turning, you should put your feet near the curves. They help you balance. Now the skateboard is moving, but you need to control the direction! The **truck**, connected to all the wheels and the deck, helps you control the deck. When you lean to the right, the truck makes the deck turn right. If you lean left, the skateboard turns left. Now what about skateboard **wheels**? Wheels do more than just move the board. They become flatter when you push down on them. And after you stop pushing down, they become round again.
>
> All three parts of the skateboard are important. So if you want to have fun, check that everything works before you go skateboarding! And don't forget your helmet, knee pads, and elbow pads!

### WB Activity 2 — True / False (AK)

1. To balance, you should stand with your feet near the curves. — **T**
2. The truck helps you control which direction you travel in. — **T**
3. Wheels just move the board. — **F**
4. When you push down on the wheels, they are still round. — **F**
5. Always check that the parts are working before you go skateboarding. — **T**

### WB Activity 3 — label the parts

`curves · deck · nose · tail · truck · wheels`

### WB Activity 4 — cause / effect chart (AK)

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| The deck is made of maple wood. | It is very strong but can bend. |
| There are curves on the deck. | You can balance more easily. |
| The truck is connected to the wheels. | You can control the direction. |
| Check that your skateboard is working. | You won't have an accident. |

**This is the design's luckiest find:** the WB's own Activity 4 is a cause/effect
chart, the same strategy the SB spread teaches. SB and WB reinforce one strategy
across two topics, so the WB mode is not a detour.
