# Design notes — Unit 3 Grammar 1 — Contrast with but and instead (`ow-l4-u3-grammar-1.html`)

## The lesson

- Our World Level 4, Unit 3 *Long Ago and Today* · SB pp. 44–45 · TR 3.4 · LP pp. 106–109 (PDF 106–109) · WB p. 29 · GWB pp. 10–11
- Objective (LP p. 106, verbatim): contrast actions now with actions in the past using but and instead
- Target pattern: **long-ago clause, but + today clause** (one sentence) and **long-ago sentence. Today sentence + instead.** (two sentences), with **too** when things are the same (SB p. 45, Activity 3)
- Academic Language: contrast · Content Vocabulary: candlelight · recycled: Vocabulary 1 (fire, sew, electric lights, cell phone…)
- **Hard scope line:** but, instead and the too of Activity 3 only. No although, however or while; comparatives wait for Grammar 2.

## The spine — The Then & Now Museum

Leo is the curator. Every exhibit is a pair — something from long ago and what we do today — and it only goes on the wall when he joins the two halves correctly with *but* or *instead* (or *too*, for the one thing that has not changed: fireplaces). The wall is on every spine slide and keeps growing (saved in the deck’s own storage). The payoff slide is the full museum, with Leo writing three exhibit labels: one with but, one with instead, one with too.

## The two bars

*Detailed*: each contrast is built in presses — first the past sentence, then the present, then the joining word in its place — so the position of *but* (middle, after a comma) and *instead* (end of the second sentence) is something Leo watches happen. The common errors (*but,* with the comma after it; *instead* twice; *instead* used like *but*) are shown broken first and fixed. *Creative*: every right contrast is an exhibit, so the wall is the visible accumulation of correct sentences — and the museum is the unit’s own idea, then and now side by side.
