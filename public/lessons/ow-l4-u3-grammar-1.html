Academic Language

contrast
/ˈkɑn-træst/ con-trast
noun/verb
a difference between two things; to show differences
🇯🇵 対比
💬 Example: But helps contrast life long ago with life today.
⚖️ To contrast is to show how two things are different. Same or different?
⚖️ Different → we contrast them with but or instead. Same → we use too.

📐 Contrast with but and instead
FormPatternExample
butpast idea + , but + present ideaLong ago, children walked to school, but today many children ride their bikes.
insteadpast idea. present/different idea + insteadMany years ago, people didn't have TV. They told stories instead.
toosame idea + tooLong ago, people had fireplaces. Today some people have fireplaces, too.
Rule: Use but for contrast inside one sentence. Use instead at the end of the second sentence. Use a comma before but. Use a period before the second sentence with instead.

Unit 3·Grammar in Depth — Where Does It Go?Lesson Planner pp. 106–109
📍 but is in the middle, after a comma. instead is at the end of the second sentence.
📍 , but … in the middle · … instead. at the end · … , too. when things are the same

Unit 3·Museum Detective 🕵️
🕵️ Every museum label has ONE mistake. Pick the fixed version.
🇯🇵 Japanese bridge: 〜だが、… = , but（文の真ん中）. 〜のかわりに… = 2つ目の文の最後に instead. 同じとき（〜も）= too.
