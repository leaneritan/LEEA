- Objective (LP p. 148, verbatim): identify and use reflexive pronouns
- Target pattern: **subject + verb + -self** when the subject and object are the same person (I cut myself), and **by + -self** = alone

- **Hard scope line:** the Student Book teaches myself, yourself, himself, herself. ourselves, yourselves, themselves and itself come in only where the Workbook and Grammar Workbook use them, on their own slide.

## The spine — The Hall of Mirrors

The planner’s material is a mirror. A reflexive pronoun is a mirror word: the action goes out and comes back to the same person. Every right -self word puts a new mirror in the hall that every spine slide shows; the payoff slide is the full hall with Leo’s own three sentences — an accident, a warning, and something he does by himself.

## The two bars

*Detailed*: each -self word is built in two presses (I → my → myself; he → him → himself), so *hisself* never looks possible; “I look at me” is shown wrong first. *Creative*: the mirror is the idea of the grammar itself, and the hall is the accumulation.
