Unit 6·Anatomy of a Poster
🧩 Four parts. Tap each card.
The ask is grammar: an obligation takes must or have to; a prohibition takes can’t. That is Grammar 1, doing real work.

Unit 6·Step 3 — Make CardsSB p. 106
🎟️ Make cards to hand out.
🐢 Sea turtles can’t tell plastic from food.
You must take your garbage home.
Beach clean-up
Saturday, 10 a.m. · South Beach
✂️ A card is a poster you can carry. Same fact, same ask — smaller, and it goes home with the person.
🚶 Written to be read while walking. Short lines. No sentence longer than a breath.
📅 Always put the clean-up details on it. The card is how people find out where to come.

Unit 6·Step 4 — Invite PeopleSB p. 107
🧹 Invite people to a community clean-up.
An invitation that misses one of these three brings nobody.
❓
WHAT
A beach clean-up to help sea animals
📍
WHERE
Name the exact spot — "South Beach, by the car park"
🕙
WHEN
Day, date and time — and when it finishes
Then add one line: what people must bring. Gloves, a bag, a hat. "You must bring gloves" — Grammar 1 again.

Unit 6·Step 5 — PhotosSB p. 107
📸
At your event, take photos for a school newspaper article.
📷 Before and after. The same spot, twice. That is the photo a newspaper prints.
👥 People working. Not posing — filling bags, carrying them, reading your poster.
🧮 Count the bags. A number in the caption turns a photo into news.
Decide the photographer now, before the day. Leo answers this in his app too — a job nobody was given is a job nobody does.

Unit 6·Be the Expert — Five Seconds
The one idea to make stick
A child who has just read four pages about coral reefs wants to put all of it on the poster. That is exactly what kills a poster.

A stranger walking past gives it five seconds. In five seconds you can land one title, one fact and one ask — and nothing more. Everything Leo cuts makes the poster stronger, not weaker.
Check it landed: hold the draft up, count to five out loud, then take it away and ask "What do I have to do?" If Dad can’t say it, the poster is not finished.
