TR 6.9
A coral reef looks like a colorful underwater garden. But corals are not plants. Corals are made up of tiny animals called . Polyps have soft, bodies without bones.
What's for dinner?
Polyps have a mouth, stomach, and to catch food. Some polyps live in warm, sunlit zones. In daylight, these polyps get food from tiny plants called . After dark, the polyps use their tentacles to catch food. Some corals can even catch fish! In contrast, polyps that live in cold, dark zones have to work harder. There are no algae, so they have to use their tentacles to catch tiny animals called in the water.
Underwater communities.
Most corals live together in huge groups called . Some build a protective skeleton around themselves. Old generations die and new ones grow on top, slowly building up coral reefs. Some are millions of years old. These beautiful reefs are home to more than 4,000 kinds of fish and thousands of other .
Save the reefs!
Coral reefs provide food for communities. They are also a source of tourism and jobs for local people. Scientists use reef animals to develop new medicines. But coral reefs are in danger from pollution. Coral reefs are disappearing. Some experts predict that only 10 percent of the world's corals will exist in the year 2050. We must protect our corals now.
🌏 Did you know? The Great Barrier Reef is more than 2,000 km (1,200 mi.) long! You can see it from outer space!

Unit 6·Be the Expert — Corals Are Animals
The one idea to make stick
A coral looks like a rock or a plant, and it does not move — so almost every child files it under "plant". The text corrects that in its second sentence: But corals are not plants.

A coral is a colony of tiny animals called polyps, each with a mouth, a stomach and tentacles. What looks like stone is the skeleton they build around themselves, layer on layer, over millions of years.
Check it landed: "Is a coral one animal or many?" — the answer is many, and that is what colony means.
