Unit 9 · The Science of Fun›Read Together ¶3
TR 9.8
📖 paragraph 3
A big circle, known as the “loop the loop,” is many people’s favorite. When you go fast up the circle, you feel heavy. Gravity is pulling you down. When you reach the top, you are high in the sky. And you’re hanging upside down! So why don’t you fall out of your seat? Your body wants to fly off, but the speed of the car and a force called centripetal force keep you moving in a circle, and keep you in your seat!
🔄 Stop at “So why don’t you fall out of your seat?” and let Leo answer before you read on.
Unit 9 · The Science of Fun›Read Together ¶4
TR 9.8
📖 paragraph 4
The roller coaster uses friction to stop. If it stops quickly, your body wants to continue moving. That is called inertia. But the safety bars keep you in place!
🛑 That is our slide 9 exactly: the car stopped, the body kept sliding. Now it has a name — inertia. And now we know why the safety bar mattered.

Unit 9 · The Science of Fun›Read Together ¶5
TR 9.8
📖 paragraph 5
Not everyone loves roller coasters. They make some people feel dizzy or sick because the forces change. In fact, we experience forces like friction, centripetal force, and gravity every day. For example, you feel gravity when you jump with your bike and come down. You feel centripetal force when you turn, and you feel friction when you use the brakes to stop the bike.
🎯 There is our first signal word in the wild: because. It points backwards at the cause.

Unit 9 · The Science of Fun›Weird but True
SB p.157
😲 Weird but True!
🧊🎢
Russians invented the roller coaster. They made the “cars” from ice. And they put straw on the seats to keep the passengers warm.
Because the cars were made of ice, the seats were freezing. As a result, they needed straw to keep people warm. Cause → effect, 400 years ago.

🎓 One more thing about cause and effect
Sometimes there is no signal word at all — the order of the sentence does the work.
“If it stops quickly, your body wants to continue moving.”
CAUSE
the coaster stops quickly
EFFECT
your body keeps moving — inertia
💬 Ask: “Where is the because in that sentence?” — there isn’t one. And it still works.
