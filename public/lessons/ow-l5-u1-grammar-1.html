🧱 Rule 1 — the shape of it

be + going to + base verb

the “be” word CHANGES

I am · he/she/it is · we/you/they are

the verb NEVER changes

going to rain ✅
going to rains ❌   going to raining ❌

Short forms: I’m · it’s · he’s · we’re · they’re going to…

🚫 Rule 4 — for a decided plan, not will

✅ SAY THIS

I’m going to go skiing this weekend.

❌ NOT THIS

I will go skiing this weekend.

📌 When the plan is already decided, English uses be going to — not will.

🇯🇵 Corner 1 — Japanese has no future tense

日本語 nihongo

明日(あした)、雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る。
Ashita, ame ga furu.

The verb 降(ふ)る furu is the SAME as for today. Only 明日 ashita tells you it is the future.

English

It is going to rain tomorrow.

English changes the verb phrase itself. You cannot just say “It rains tomorrow.” ❌

⚠️ This is why the future is hard: Japanese marks it with a time word, English marks it with grammar. You must do BOTH in English.

🇯🇵 Corner 2 — つもり tsumori and そう sō

📅 PLAN — you decided

スキーに行(い)くつもりです。
Sukī ni iku tsumori desu.

→ I’m going to go skiing.

🔮 PREDICTION — from a clue

雨(あめ)が降(ふ)りそうです。
Ame ga furisō desu.

→ It’s going to rain.

🎯 Japanese uses two different endings. English uses one form — be going to — for both. That is good news: one thing to learn, not two! 🎉

🇯🇵 Corner 3 — the be word has no Japanese partner

In Japanese the verb does not change for I / you / he / they. In English the be word does — and it is the piece Japanese learners drop most often.
