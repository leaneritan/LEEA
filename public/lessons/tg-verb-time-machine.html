The Verb Time Machine

Infinitive ・ Simple Past ・ Past Participle

Today we learn the three forms of every English verb — and the one big secret that makes the third form easy.

🇯🇵 タップして日本語で見る
英語の動詞（どうし）には3つの形があります。
今日はその3つの違いと、3つ目「過去分詞（かこぶんし）」が何のためにあるのかを、サッカーと映画の例で学びます。
Why three forms? 🤔

🇯🇵 Japanese has 2

食べる  →  食べた
行く  →  行った

Present form, past form. Two shapes and you're done.

🇬🇧 English has 3

eat  →  ate  →  eaten
go  →  went  →  gone

The third shape has no simple Japanese match. That's why it feels strange — and that's exactly what we'll fix today.

The third form is called the past participle. It is not a tense. It is a part — a piece you snap onto a helper word to build something bigger. 🧩

🇯🇵 タップ
日本語は「食べる / 食べた」の2つですが、英語は3つ目があります。
この3つ目「過去分詞」は時制（じせい）ではありません。単独では時間を表さず、他の言葉とくっついて初めて意味を持つ「部品（ぶひん）」です。だから一人では使えないのです。

The past participle has exactly TWO jobs:

Job 1 · have / has + eaten  →  "I have eaten." (experience / just finished)
Job 2 · was / were + kicked  →  "The ball was kicked." (someone did it to it)
Form 1 — Infinitive 原形

The verb's real name

This is the form you find in the dictionary. No -ed, no -s, no -ing. Just the plain word.

play ⚽   watch 🎬   score 🥅   win 🏆   go 🚗   eat 🍞

Leo, you already use this form every day — it's the word that comes after want to and like to.

I want to play soccer.
Leo likes to watch Marvel movies.
🇯🇵 タップ
原形は辞書に載っている「もとの形」です。-ed も -s も -ing もつきません。
"want to ~" や "like to ~" のあとに使う形なので、レオはもう毎日使っています！

5 places the infinitive appears 📍

1. After to → I want to go.
2. After can / will / must → He can run fast.
3. After do / does → Do you like soccer?
4. After did → I didn't go. ⚠️ not "didn't went"!
5. As a command → Pass the ball!
🇯🇵 タップ
特に4番が大事！
"did" や "didn't" が文にあるとき、動詞は原形にもどります。
❌ I didn't went. → ✅ I didn't go.
過去を表す仕事は "did" がもうやっているので、動詞は原形でいいのです。
Form 2 — Simple Past 過去形

It happened. It's finished. ✅

Simple past puts an action at a finished point in the past. The action is over. We are looking back at it like a photo. 📸

Messi played for Argentina yesterday.
Leo watched John Wick last night.
Arsenal won the title in 2026.

Notice: each sentence has a finished time — yesterday, last night, in 2026.

🇯🇵 タップ
過去形は「過去のある時点で終わったこと」を表します。日本語の「〜した」とほぼ同じです。
いつ起きたかがはっきりしている（yesterday, last night, in 2026 など）のが特徴です。
🕰️ ➡️ 📸

Simple past time words

yesterday · last night · last week
in 2026 · two days ago · when I was 8
this morning (if morning is over)

If you can point at when, use simple past.

Simple Past — Spelling the -ed ✍️

Rule 1 · Most verbs → add -ed

play → played · watch → watched · kick → kicked

Rule 2 · Ends in e → add only -d

live → lived · score → scored · like → liked

Rule 3 · Short vowel + 1 consonant → double it

stop → stopped · plan → planned · drop → dropped

Rule 4 · Consonant + y → y becomes i

study → studied · try → tried · carry → carried
But: play → played (vowel + y, no change!)
🇯🇵 タップ
① ふつうは -ed をつける
② e で終わる語は -d だけ
③ 「短い母音＋子音1つ」で終わる語は子音を2つに（stop→stopped）
④ 「子音＋y」は y を i にして -ied（study→studied）。ただし「母音＋y」はそのまま（play→played）。
Simple Past — The 3 sounds of -ed 👄

"-ed" is written the same way, but it has three different sounds. This is where Japanese speakers get caught. 🎯

/t/

Sounds like "t"

watched → "watcht"
kicked → "kickt"
stoped → "stopt"

After quiet sounds: k, p, s, ch, sh, f

/d/

Sounds like "d"

played → "playd"
scored → "scord"
lived → "livd"

After noisy sounds & vowels: b, g, l, m, n, r, v

/ɪd/

Extra syllable "id"

wanted → "want-id"
needed → "need-id"
started → "start-id"

ONLY after t or d

🇯🇵 タップ
日本語話者がやりがちなミス：全部「〜エッド」と読んでしまうこと。
実際は3種類だけ：
・t や d で終わる動詞だけ「イド」と1音節ふえる（wanted =「ウォンティッド」）
・それ以外は音がふえません。watched は「ウォッチト」、played は「プレイド」。
👄 口のヒント：watched を言うとき、最後は舌を上の歯ぐきにつけて「ト」と切るだけ。母音は入れません。
Simple Past — Irregular verbs 不規則動詞

About 200 English verbs refuse to take -ed. They change shape instead. There is no rule — but there are patterns, and patterns make memorizing 3× easier. 💪

go → went    see → saw
take → took    win → won

Bad news: they're the most common verbs in English, so you can't avoid them.
Good news: because they're common, you'll hear them constantly and they'll stick.

🇯🇵 タップ
不規則動詞は -ed をつけず、形そのものが変わります。約200語ありますが、よく使うのは60語くらい。
ルールはありませんが「パターン（型）」があります。バラバラに覚えるより、型ごとにまとめて覚えるほうが3倍ラクです。

⭐ KEY IDEA 1
🧍

Simple past can stand ALONE

It doesn't need any helper word. Put it after the subject and the sentence is finished.

Leo ate breakfast. complete ✅
Mbappé scored 25 goals in La Liga. complete ✅
We went to the stadium. complete ✅
🇯🇵 タップ
過去形はそれだけで文が完成します。have や was などの助けの言葉はいりません。
「主語＋過去形」でもう文になります。これが次に習う過去分詞との一番大きな違いです。
⚠️ Careful — the "did" trap

When you make a question or a negative in the past, the word did carries the past. So the verb goes back to the infinitive.

✗ Wrong

✗ Did you went to the game?
✗ I didn't saw the movie.
✗ He didn't played yesterday.

✓ Right

✓ Did you go to the game?
✓ I didn't see the movie.
✓ He didn't play yesterday.

🧠 Think of it this way: only one word gets to be past. If "did" is there, "did" is the past one — the main verb rests. 😴

🇯🇵 タップ
疑問文・否定文では "did" が過去を表す仕事をします。
だから動詞は原形にもどります。「過去を表すのは1語だけ」と覚えましょう。
❌ I didn't went. → ✅ I didn't go.（did がもう過去だから、go はそのまま）
Form 3 — Past Participle 過去分詞

The one that's not a tense 🧩

The past participle does not tell time by itself. It's a puzzle piece. You snap it onto a helper word, and together they make a meaning.

What does it look like?

Regular verbs → exactly the same as simple past
play → played → played   (easy! 😄)
Irregular verbs → often a third, different word
go → went → gone
see → saw → seen
eat → ate → eaten
🇯🇵 タップ
過去分詞はそれだけでは時間を表しません。パズルのピースのようなもので、助けの言葉とくっついて初めて意味ができます。
規則動詞なら過去形とまったく同じ形。不規則動詞は go→went→gone のように3つ目の形になることが多いです。
🧩 ➕ 🤝

Helper words it snaps onto

have / has / had + participle
am / is / are / was / were + participle

Without a helper, a past participle is just a piece lying on the floor. It can't be a sentence. 🚫

⭐ KEY IDEA 2 — the most important rule
🚫🧍

Past participle NEVER stands alone

✓ Leo has eaten breakfast.
✓ The ball was kicked into the net.
✗ I gone to the game.  no helper — broken!
✗ He seen the movie.  no helper — broken!
🇯🇵 タップ
過去分詞は絶対に一人では使えません。必ず have / has / had か am / is / are / was / were と一緒に使います。
❌ I gone to the game. は、部品だけ置いてあって文になっていない状態です。
✅ I have gone / I went のどちらかにする必要があります。
Job 1 — have / has + participle

"I have eaten." 🍞

This is called the present perfect. It connects the past to now. It has two main meanings:

Meaning A · Experience 経験

I have been to England. → sometime in my life
Leo has seen Avatar three times.

Meaning B · Just finished / result now 完了

I have finished my homework. → so I'm free now!
Arsenal have won the title. → they are champions now

🇯🇵 The Japanese bridge

Experience → 「〜したことがある」
I have been to England.
= イギリスに行ったことがある。
Just finished → 「〜してしまった / もう〜した」
I have finished.
= もう終わった。
🇯🇵 もっと見る
present perfect（現在完了）は「過去」と「今」をつなぐ形です。
have/has ＋ 過去分詞 で作ります。
ポイント：いつやったかは言いません。大事なのは「今どうなっているか」だからです。
⚖️ The big comparison
Simple Past
I ate breakfast at 7:00.

📸 A photo of a finished moment.
We say WHEN. The time is over and closed.

Leo watched John Wick last night.
Messi went to Paris in 2021.
Present Perfect
I have eaten breakfast.

🔗 A line from the past to now.
We don't say when. We care about now.

Leo has watched John Wick. (he knows the story)
Messi has won the World Cup. (he's a winner now)

🔑 The test: If the sentence says when (yesterday, last night, in 2021) → simple past. If it does not say when, and you care about the result now → present perfect.

🇯🇵 タップ
日本語だとどちらも「食べた」になるので混ざりやすい部分です。
・「7時に食べた」→ いつが言ってある → simple past（I ate at 7:00）
・「（もう）食べた」→ いつは言わない、今おなかがいっぱい → present perfect（I have eaten）
⚠️ ❌ I have eaten at 7:00. は間違い。「いつ」を言った瞬間、simple past にしなければなりません。
Time words tell you which one 🚦
→ Simple Past
  • yesterday · last night · last week
  • in 2026 · two days ago
  • when I was 8 · this morning (finished)
  • after the game · at 7:00
Leo played soccer yesterday.
→ Present Perfect (have + pp)
  • already · yet · just
  • ever · never
  • so far · up to now
  • three times · many times
Leo has played soccer many times.
🇯🇵 タップ
時を表す言葉が「合図（あいず）」になります。
yesterday や last night が見えたら → 過去形
already, just, ever, never, ~times が見えたら → have/has ＋ 過去分詞
この合図を覚えるだけで、正解率がぐっと上がります。
Job 2 — be + participle (passive)

"The ball was kicked." ⚽

Normally the subject does the action:

Mbappé kicked the ball.  → Mbappé does it.

But sometimes we want to talk about the ball, not the player. Then we use be + past participle:

The ball was kicked.  → the ball receives it.
The movie was made in 2008. 🎬
The goal was scored in the 90th minute. 🥅
Fossils are found in rocks. 🦴

🇯🇵 The Japanese bridge

passive = 受け身（うけみ）「〜される / 〜された」
The ball was kicked.
= ボールがけられた。
The movie was made in 2008.
= その映画は2008年に作られた。
🇯🇵 もっと見る
日本語の受け身「〜られる／〜された」と同じ考え方です。
英語では be動詞 ＋ 過去分詞 で作ります。
だから過去分詞は「現在完了」と「受け身」の両方で必要になる、とても働き者の形なのです。

🇯🇵 タップ
迷ったらこの3ステップ：
① 文に have/has/was/were などの助けの言葉がある？ → 過去分詞
② 助けの言葉がない？ → 過去形
③ did / didn't がある？ → 原形
これだけで、ほとんどの問題が解けます。

Pattern: i → a → u 🎵

The "-en" family 🔚  Many participles just end in -en:

eat / ate / eaten  ·  take / took / taken  ·  write / wrote / written  ·  give / gave / given  ·  break / broke / broken  ·  speak / spoke / spoken
🇯🇵 タップ
バラバラに覚えず、型（パターン）でまとめて覚えるのがコツです。
特に「i → a → u」型（sing/sang/sung）と「-en で終わる」型（eaten, taken, written）を覚えると、一気にたくさんの動詞が使えるようになります。
🚨 The 5 mistakes to avoid
✗ I gone to the stadium.  →  ✓ I went / I have gone. (participle needs a helper)
✗ He has went home.  →  ✓ He has gone home. (after "has" use the participle, not the past)
✗ I didn't saw it.  →  ✓ I didn't see it. ("did" already carries the past)
✗ I have eaten at 7:00.  →  ✓ I ate at 7:00. (if you say WHEN, use simple past)
✗ The ball was kick.  →  ✓ The ball was kicked. (after "was" use the participle)
🇯🇵 タップ
この5つが日本人学習者のミスのほとんどです。特に②「has went」と④「have eaten at 7:00」は中学・高校でもよく出ます。
覚え方：have/has のあとは必ず過去分詞／「いつ」を言ったら過去形。

Recap & Wrap Up 🏆

The three forms

  • Infinitive — the real name: play, go, eat. Used after to, can, did.
  • Simple Past — finished, says WHEN, stands alone: played, went, ate.
  • Past Participle — never alone, always with a helper: played, gone, eaten.

The participle's two jobs

  • have / has + pp → present perfect
    "I have eaten." 「もう食べた／食べたことがある」
  • be + pp → passive
    "The ball was kicked." 「ボールがけられた」

🔑 If you remember only one thing today: Simple past stands alone. Past participle needs a friend. 🧍 vs 🤝
